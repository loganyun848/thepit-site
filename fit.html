How do I measure?

01 / RECONSTRUCTION REFERENCES

Four views. One consistent reference.

Front 0° · Left 90° · Back 180° · Right 270°. Left and right refer to the person in the image.

1 / 4

Add three views to your approved front image: left, back and right. The same A-pose, framing and outfit guide the 3D reconstruction. Explore camera angles on the completed model without further AI generation charges.

Check your front reference before continuing.

Select a view to enlarge or save it. Side and back views infer areas missing from the original photo; they are not a precise body scan. Check that the body, hands, feet and outfit are consistent across all four views. If the feet are cropped, return to Step 01 and upload a full-body photo.

02 / DIGITAL DOUBLE

One model. Every angle.

Reconstruct a textured 3D mesh from four views. Camera framing is calculated from the model dimensions.

3D generation is billed separately from image generation. Rotation, camera changes and Blender export do not trigger additional AI generation charges. Submitted jobs are not automatically regenerated.

Start after checking all four full-body references. 3D generation may take several minutes.

NEXT / MOTION PREVIEW

Bring your avatar into motion.

Our studio program adds a body rig and a five-second wave, then saves an MP4 and an editable Blender project for review. Set the correct front direction above before sending.

No additional AI generation credits. Processing starts when our production workstation is online. Motion previews require human review; they are not automatically published.

The Blender file includes the mesh, UVs, packed PBR textures, 12 cameras and lights. This is a static model with no skeleton, facial expressions or motion. Likeness, hands, hair and unseen surfaces require further review and refinement for production use.

Changing your photo, outfit or face reference, or regenerating the front image, clears the previous sheet. Keep this page open during generation and save results before leaving. The 3D mesh is static; motion and facial animation require separate rigging and review.
